Burial comes first. The bill shouldn't.

In our tradition, burial happens quickly — often within a day. That leaves no time to fundraise, apply for assistance, or wait on a decision. Families are asked for [TYPICAL FUNERAL COST] at the exact moment they are least able to think about money.

UUFFUNDS exists so that money is already there. Not as charity, and not as insurance — as a standing agreement between neighbours to carry each other's costs.

How it works

Four steps, and nothing hidden in between.

1

You join

One household, one membership. You register your details and a payment method, and you are covered from [WHEN COVERAGE BEGINS].

2

A death is reported

The family, or the funeral home acting for them, notifies the fund. Paperwork such as a death certificate or burial permit can be supplied by the funeral home, but is not required before the fund acts — burial is never delayed waiting on documents.

3

The cost is split

The funeral cost is divided across all active members. Each member is charged their share — roughly [SHARE PER FUNERAL] — for that funeral.

4

The bill gets paid

Funds go to [THE FUNERAL HOME / THE FAMILY] directly. No claim forms for the family to file, and no reimbursement to chase.

What you're agreeing to

This is a shared obligation, not a policy you buy. Please read this part carefully before you join — we would rather you join knowing exactly how it works than leave when the first charge arrives.

You are charged per funeral, not per month

There is no fixed monthly fee and no cap on how often a charge can come. Each death is a separate request. In a difficult month there may be several; in a quiet month, none at all.

If someone's payment fails, the rest of us cover it

When a member's payment does not go through, their share is redistributed across the remaining members. This is what keeps the family whole, and it means your share can end up slightly higher than the original estimate.

Your share shrinks as the fund grows

The cost is divided by the number of active members. The larger the membership, the smaller each individual share becomes.

This is mutual aid, not insurance
